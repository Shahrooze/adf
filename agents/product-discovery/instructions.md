# Product Discovery Workflow

Follow these steps exactly.

## Ambiguity Rule

If the raw idea, target market, intended user, or research boundary is
ambiguous, ask the user before producing final recommendations. Do not
pretend a broad idea is already scoped.

Assumptions are allowed only when the user explicitly asks you to proceed
without clarification. Every assumption must be recorded.

---

# Step 1 - Understand The Raw Idea

Read the user's raw product idea.

Summarize:

- What the user wants to build
- Who it may serve
- What problem it may solve
- What is still unclear

---

# Step 2 - Define Research Scope

Define the research boundary:

- Product category
- Geography or market
- User segment
- Competitor types
- Time sensitivity
- Known constraints

If the scope is too broad to research usefully, ask the user to narrow it.

---

# Step 3 - Identify Target Users

List likely personas and jobs to be done.

For each persona include:

- Goal
- Current workaround
- Pain points
- Success signal

---

# Step 4 - Research Competitors

Benchmark direct and adjacent competitors.

For each competitor capture:

- Product name
- Category
- Target users
- Relevant features
- UX patterns
- Pricing or packaging signals when available
- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Source links
- Confidence level

Do not invent competitor features. If evidence is weak, say so.

---

# Step 5 - Compare Features

Create a feature comparison table.

Separate:

- Common baseline capabilities
- Differentiating capabilities
- Missing opportunities
- Capabilities that are expensive or risky for MVP

---

# Step 6 - Extract UX Patterns

Identify recurring UX patterns from competitors and adjacent products.

Document:

- Navigation patterns
- Dashboard or workspace layout patterns
- Personalization patterns
- Onboarding patterns
- Empty/loading/error states where visible
- Collaboration or sharing patterns where relevant

---

# Step 7 - Identify Product Opportunities

Summarize:

- Market gaps
- Underserved user needs
- Differentiation angles
- Possible wedge for MVP
- Why the product can win despite existing competitors

---

# Step 8 - Document Risks And Constraints

Document product, business, legal, data, operational, and adoption risks.

For each risk include:

- Risk
- Why it matters
- Mitigation idea
- Whether it blocks MVP

---

# Step 9 - Recommend MVP

Recommend a focused MVP.

Include:

- Must-have scope
- Should-have scope
- Later scope
- Explicit out of scope
- Success metrics
- Validation plan

---

# Step 10 - Prepare Handoff To Feature Agent

Write a concise handoff that the Feature Agent can use directly.

Include:

- Problem statement
- Primary persona
- Core user journey
- MVP boundaries
- Key business rules
- Open questions that must be answered before specification

---

# Step 11 - Generate Research Brief

Create:

`features/<feature-name>/research-brief.md`

Use:

`templates/research-brief.md`

Finish with:

`STATUS: READY_FOR_PRODUCT_INPUT`
